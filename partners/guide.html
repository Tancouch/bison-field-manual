Which door for which buyer

Where to start when you're sitting across from them. We'll narrow it from there.

First-time buyer

Conventional 3%, HomeReady, FHA

Credit decides between them. Above roughly 680, conventional usually wins on MI.

Short on cash

FHA, VA, USDA, seller credits

Ask about gift funds early. The paper trail takes time.

Veteran

VA, always check first

Zero down and no MI. Tell the listing agent it closes on time.

Self-employed

Bank Statement, P&L, 1099

If their tax returns show low income, don't stop. Call us.

Investor

DSCR, Conventional investment

DSCR doesn't care how many properties they own or what they earn.

Move-up

Conventional, HELOC for the bridge

Pulling equity from the current home can let them buy before they sell.

Luxury

Jumbo, High Balance, ARM

Reserves and documentation matter more than rate. Start early.

Retiree

Asset Depletion, Conventional

Big assets but low reported income is approvable. Declined elsewhere is not declined here.

Fixer-upper

203(k), HomeStyle

Lets them win the house nobody else can finance.

The 60-second pre-qual

Five questions at the first showing. They tell you whether this buyer is ready, and they tell us where to start.

  1. "Have you talked to a lender yet, and do you have a letter?"No letter, no offers. Get them to us the same day.
  2. "What monthly payment feels comfortable, all in?"Their number, not their max. Price range follows from it.
  3. "How do you get paid: W-2, self-employed, commission, retirement?"Anything other than W-2 changes the program.
  4. "What are you planning to put down, and where is it coming from?"Gifts, 401(k) loans, and a home sale all need a plan.
  5. "Anything we should know about credit: recent late payments, collections, a new car?"Better to hear it now than in underwriting.

Red flags: send these to us early

None of these kills a deal if we hear about it before the offer. All of them can if we hear about it in escrow.

New credit during escrow

Car, furniture, a new card. Credit gets pulled again before closing. Tell buyers: no new debt until they have keys.

Large unexplained deposits

Anything that isn't payroll must be documented. Cash can't be. Gifts need a letter and a paper trail.

Job change

A new employer, a switch to 1099, or a resignation near closing triggers re-underwriting.

Condos

The building has to qualify, not just the buyer. Pending lawsuits and low reserves in the HOA stop files.

Insurance

In fire and wind zones, getting a policy bound can take longer than the loan. Start at contract.

Low appraisal risk

Multiple offers well over list? Talk through the appraisal gap plan before you write it.

Part 02 · In the Deal

WHAT TO SAY
AND WHEN

Scripts for the money conversations buyers bring to you first. Point buyers in the right direction, then hand them to us for specific rates and terms.

Rate and payment scripts

"What's the rate right now?"
"It depends on your credit, your down payment, and the type of home, so the number you see online usually isn't yours. Let me get you ten minutes with my lender and you'll have your actual number today."

Don't quote a rate. A wrong number, even a good one, is the one the buyer remembers.

"What will my payment be?"
"Let's look at the whole payment: loan, taxes, insurance, and mortgage insurance if it applies. HOA dues come on top of that. That's the number that hits your account every month, so it's the one we should shop by."

Buyers who only hear principal and interest feel misled at closing. The Field Manual calculator shows the full payment.

"I saw a lower rate online."
"You probably did. Those ads assume top-tier credit, 25 percent down, and often a point paid up front. Send it to my lender. If it really applies to you, they'll tell you straight."

Agree first, then explain. Getting defensive loses the buyer.

"Should we wait for rates to drop?"
"You can refinance later if rates fall. You can't go back and buy this house at this price. And when rates drop, more buyers show up and prices tend to move. Let's see what the payment looks like today and decide from there."

Don't promise that a refinance will happen or what it will save. Present it as an option they'll have.

Writing a stronger offer with your lender

  1. Get a fully reviewed pre-approval, not a pre-qual.We review documents up front, so the letter holds up when a listing agent calls.
  2. Have us call the listing agent.A 3-minute call from the lender saying "this buyer is solid, we close in house" wins more multiple-offer situations than an extra $2,000.
  3. Ask for a buydown instead of a price cut.A seller credit toward a temporary buydown usually lowers the buyer's payment more than the same dollars off the price. We'll run both options side by side.
  4. Plan for the appraisal gap before you write it.Know how much cash the buyer can bring if the appraisal comes in low, and write the offer to that number.
  5. Write a closing date you can actually hit.Check with us first. A short close we can deliver beats an aggressive date we have to extend.

Part 04 · Sales

FILL THE
PIPELINE

Most business comes from people who already know you. The plays below are how you get those people to act.

Lead generation plays

Open houses

Show real numbers

Bring a flyer with the full monthly payment at a few down payment levels. Buyers who aren't pre-approved yet leave with a reason to call you back.

Sphere

Ten conversations a day

Not a pitch. "Who do you know thinking about moving this year?" asked often enough fills the calendar.

Past clients

Equity reviews

Once a year, show each past client what their home is worth now and what that equity could do for them. It starts move-up and investor conversations.

Social

Teach, don't list

One short video a week answering a buyer question. Listings get scrolled past. Answers get saved and shared.

Renters

Rent vs. own

A first-time buyer workshop: an agent, a lender, 45 minutes. Many renters don't know 3% down exists.

Investors

Rental numbers

Bring DSCR-ready deals to investors in your sphere. They buy more than once.

Things we can do together

Co-marketing with a lender is allowed when it is done correctly. We follow the rules on every piece.

You cover finding the home, we cover financing. Held quarterly at your office or online.

Listing payment flyers

For your listings: the full monthly payment at several down payments, plus buydown scenarios.

We can answer financing questions on the spot at your open houses.

Your name on the Field Manual, to give to your own clients.

How we keep it clean: shared marketing costs are split in proportion to each party's share of the advertising, and nothing of value is ever exchanged for a referral. That protects both of our licenses.

Converting online leads

  1. Call within five minutes.Response time matters more than anything else. An automatic text buys you the five minutes. It doesn't replace the call.
  2. Ask about them before the house."What's got you looking?" gets the real story. "Which property?" gets a one-word answer.
  3. Offer something useful."Want me to send the full monthly payment on that one?" earns a reply.
  4. Follow up for 30 days.Day 1: call and text. Days 2, 3, 5, 7: alternate texts and calls. Then weekly. Most online leads convert after several contacts, not on the first one.
  5. Get them to a lender early.A buyer who's pre-approved has made a commitment. Everyone else is still browsing.

Asking for referrals

At closing
"I work mostly by referral, and the way I grow is by taking care of people you care about. When someone you know starts talking about moving, would you be comfortable introducing us?"
At a follow-up
"Quick question: who's the last person you heard mention buying or selling? I'd love to be a resource for them, even if it's just a question."
After a thank-you
"Thank you, that means a lot. The best compliment you could give me is passing my name to a friend."

Ask at the moment they're happiest: keys in hand, a closing gift, a good review.
